# BABAI Field Research

Research date: September 2026

## Summary

We spoke with 19 businesses:

- 15 restaurants
- 4 stores

The feedback was strongly positive. Several businesses are actively dissatisfied with food aggregators, want direct customer relationships, or already receive WhatsApp orders but struggle to manage them.

This is the first meaningful validation signal for BABAI. It does not yet prove willingness to pay, but it validates that the problem is real and urgent enough to create interest.

## Discovery pitch

> Bro, how are you managing your orders and giving customers a good experience while maintaining profits?

## Customer pain observed

### Rasaangi

Last month, the business generated approximately ₹1.57 lakh through an aggregator but received only ₹54,000. They reported that marketing charges were deducted even after they had turned marketing off. They said the aggregator re-enabled it within approximately 30 minutes, and customer support later stated that the charges would remain unless a bug was proven.

### Chantabbai

The business generated approximately ₹15 lakh but received only ₹8 lakh. Customers ask whether they can place orders through calls or WhatsApp. The business built its own application, but because the team is not technical, development had to be outsourced.

### Brew Vista Cafe

The cafe wants to leave aggregators because the remaining margin is insufficient.

### Maduram

The business already has its own application and wants to encourage customers to use it for better prices.

### Local store

Customers message the store through WhatsApp, but staff management is difficult. Staff members cannot comfortably read English while customers frequently write in English, forcing the owner to translate conversations.

## Proposed BABAI solution

BABAI would let the business remain on WhatsApp while providing:

- A conversational assistant for customer questions
- Menu/catalog understanding
- Order capture and confirmation
- Direct payment to the business
- Staff takeover and assignment
- Customer support through the business’s own staff
- Optional delivery coordination
- No customer app installation
- No per-order commission

The proposed money flow is:

```text
Customer → Restaurant or store
Business → BABAI subscription
```

BABAI should not hold or settle customer funds in the initial product.

## Pricing hypothesis

Initial subscription plans discussed:

- Basic: ₹999/month before GST
- Premium: ₹2,499/month before GST
- Advanced: ₹4,999/month before GST

There may also be a one-time onboarding and training fee covering:

- Setup
- Menu/catalog ingestion
- Configuration
- Staff onboarding
- Approximately two hours of training

These prices are hypotheses and must be tested through paid pilots.

## Customer reaction

Businesses responded positively to the following proposition:

> Your customers can order through your own WhatsApp. The assistant handles routine conversation and order confirmation, the money goes directly to you, there is no marketplace middleman, and your customers do not need to install another app.

Several businesses asked whether they could get started immediately. This should be treated as strong interest, but not yet as paid validation.

## What this validates

The research supports these hypotheses:

1. Aggregator commissions and unexplained charges are a serious pain point.
2. Businesses want direct customer relationships.
3. Customers already prefer familiar channels such as WhatsApp and phone calls.
4. Businesses do not want to build or maintain complex technology themselves.
5. A subscription model is easier to understand than another transaction commission.
6. Staff takeover is necessary; automation alone is not enough.
7. Pickup-first ordering is a credible starting workflow.
8. The same underlying problem may later apply to other local businesses such as stores and pharmacies.

## What remains unvalidated

Positive conversation is not yet proof that businesses will:

- Provide their WhatsApp number and menu
- Change their existing operating habits
- Let customers use the system during busy periods
- Pay the proposed subscription
- Pay onboarding fees
- Trust automated order handling
- Continue using the system after the first trial

Delivery, payment verification, refunds, message reliability, Meta onboarding, and multilingual staff workflows also require real-world testing.

## Immediate pilot plan

Select three businesses from the 19 interviews and run a narrow pilot:

```text
Connect business WhatsApp
→ upload/review/publish menu or catalog
→ customer asks questions
→ customer places pickup order
→ business accepts or rejects
→ payment is recorded or confirmed
→ staff completes the order
→ customer receives status
```

The pilot should start with one business, one WhatsApp number, one branch, pickup-first fulfillment, manual human takeover, and no delivery dependency.

Ask each candidate for:

- Menu/catalog
- WhatsApp number or approved test channel
- Primary operating contact
- Staff member responsible for orders
- Current weekly WhatsApp order volume
- Current aggregator/direct-order economics
- Willingness to pay a pilot fee or refundable deposit

## Success criteria

The next validation stage should measure:

- Number of businesses that commit to a pilot
- Number that provide real menus and operating access
- Onboarding time
- Menu correction rate
- Customer order conversion
- Staff handling time
- Missed or duplicate messages
- Payment confirmation problems
- Human takeover frequency
- Repeat orders
- Support effort
- Actual Meta, AI, infrastructure, and payment costs
- Conversion from pilot to paid subscription

The strongest proof would be three active pilot businesses and at least one paying customer.

## Strategic conclusion

BABAI should not expand its architecture immediately based only on positive interviews. It should convert the 19 conversations into three pilots and at least one paid customer.

The strongest initial positioning is:

> Get direct WhatsApp orders from your own customers without marketplace commission or forcing customers to install another app.

Restaurants remain the first wedge. Stores and other local businesses should be treated as later expansion opportunities until the restaurant/pickup workflow is proven.
